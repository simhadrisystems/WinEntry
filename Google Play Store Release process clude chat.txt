⏺ Glad the policy is looking good. My honest view on the over-explanation
  concern, then I'll add the Section 6 summary.

  ---
  My assessment — the risk is real

  The current policy is trying to serve two audiences at once: a user wanting to
   know what happens to their data, and a Google reviewer needing architectural
  justification for technical choices. In Google's current scrutiny environment,
   that dual-audience approach creates rejection risk rather than reducing it,
  for three specific reasons:

  1. Internal function names are a liability. syncUserSheet,
  deleteUserRegistration — if a reviewer checks these against the actual Cloud
  Functions deployed (which they can do via the OAuth app verification process),
   any mismatch is an immediate flag. Policy language should describe behaviour,
   not implementation.
  2. Defensive phrasing signals something to defend. The paragraph we just added
   says "a privacy benefit rather than a workaround" — but saying "rather than a
   workaround" draws the reviewer's eye to the question of whether it might be a
   workaround. Clean user-facing language wouldn't mention it at all.
  3. Infrastructure specifics create maintenance obligations. asia-south1 region
   — if you ever add a second region or migrate, the policy is technically
  wrong. Reviewers can reject for stale infrastructure claims.

  What should stay: what data is collected, why, user controls, the verbatim
  Limited Use statement, audit log retention with "security and fraud
  prevention."

  What should be trimmed: function names, region name, "proxy/intermediary"
  architectural language — replaced with plain "secure server-side process." The
   spreadsheets scope table row can state the two user-visible operations
  without explaining the service account mechanism.

  I'd recommend a cleanup pass after this. Want me to proceed?

  Google presents a form with a text field for each sensitive scope you've

  Where exactly: Google Cloud Console → APIs & Services → OAuth consent screen →
   "Submit for Verification" → the form asks per-scope: "Explain how your 
  application uses this scope and why a less sensitive scope is insufficient."

  There's no special format — just clear, honest prose. Character limit is

  ---
  Scope: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/spreadsheets

  Justification:

  WinEntry is a retail inventory management application. Cloud backup is an
  optional, user-initiated feature that stores inventory records in a Google
  Sheets workspace provisioned and managed by the administrator (Simhadri
  Systems). The spreadsheets scope is requested exclusively for two
  user-visible, user-initiated operations described below. It is not used for
  routine data sync.

  Operation 1 — Account Registration
  Path: Settings → Business Info → Save & Register

  When a user opts in to cloud backup, they submit a workspace request by
  tapping "Save & Register". At this point the app uses the spreadsheets scope
  to write the user's registration details (business name and any optional
  fields the user has filled in) to the administrator's registration registry
  spreadsheet. This is a one-time write, triggered explicitly by the user. The
  result is immediately visible: the registration status in the app updates to
  "✓ Registered". Without this write, the administrator cannot identify the
  user's account or provision a workspace for them.

  Operation 2 — Account Deletion
  Path: Settings → Business Info → ⋮ menu → Delete Account & Data

  When a user chooses to permanently delete their account, the app uses the
  spreadsheets scope to: (a) clear all inventory records from the user's
  assigned cloud workspace spreadsheet, and (b) blank the user's personal
  information from the administrator's registry row. This operation is
  irreversible and initiated entirely by the user. The result is immediately
  visible: the app signs the user out and confirms deletion. Without this scope,
   the app cannot fulfill the user's right to delete their own cloud data.

  Why routine sync does not use this scope:
  All routine inventory sync (daily stock entries, purchases, day-end summaries)
   is performed by a secure server-side process managed by the administrator,
  using administrator-held credentials. The user's OAuth token is not involved
  in routine sync. This means the spreadsheets scope is requested at sign-in but
   lies dormant until one of the two user-initiated operations above occurs.

  Why a less sensitive scope is insufficient:
  The two operations above require writing to and reading from specific
  spreadsheets owned by the administrator's Google account. The spreadsheets
  scope is the minimum scope that permits read/write access to specific
  spreadsheets across account boundaries (administrator-owned sheets accessed on
   behalf of the user). A read-only scope would not permit the registration
  write or the data deletion.

  Scope of access — what is never accessed:
  The app never accesses the user's personal Google Drive files, Gmail,
  Calendar, Contacts, or any other Google service. It does not access any
  spreadsheets the user has personally created. Access is strictly limited to
  the two administrator-managed sheets described above.

  Privacy policy reference:
  Full disclosure of this scope's use is documented in Section 4 ("Google API
  Scopes Used") and Section 5 ("How Your Data Is Used") of the WinEntry Privacy
  Policy at: https://sites.google.com/view/winentryapp/privacy
